1. Core HRMS modules at a glance

1. Core HR and employee information management

The central employee database, employment history, organizational structure and personnel records.




2. Time, attendance and leave management

Attendance capture, shift scheduling, overtime, leave balances and absence management.




3. Payroll, compensation and benefits

Salary calculations, allowances, deductions, statutory contributions and payroll reconciliation.




4. Recruitment and onboarding

Vacancies, applications, interviews, hiring approvals, offers and new employee orientation.




5. Performance and talent management

Appraisals, objectives, competencies, career progression, succession and development.




6. Analytics, employee self-service and HR automation

Dashboards, reporting, workflow approvals, notifications, service requests and decision support.


These are six functional groups, not six separate applications. A mature HRMS connects them through a shared employee record and workflow engine.

A complete enterprise system should also cover the specialized modules below.

3. Recruitment and applicant tracking system (ATS)
This module manages recruitment from the initial request for a new employee to the candidate's acceptance of an offer.
Feature	Purpose
Manpower requisitions	Managers request additional staff and explain the business need.
Recruitment approvals	Route vacancies through authorized managers, HR and finance.
Vacancy and job posting management	Create job advertisements and publish them to approved channels.
Candidate portal	Allow applicants to submit applications, CVs and supporting documents.
Applicant tracking	Track each candidate through application, screening, interviews and selection.
CV and qualification screening	Record qualifications, relevant experience and role requirements.
Interview scheduling	Coordinate interview dates, panels, venues and candidate notifications.
Interview scorecards	Standardize assessment criteria and capture panel feedback.
Reference and background checks	Record authorized verification and its status.
Candidate communication	Send acknowledgments, interview invitations, updates and outcome notifications.
Offer management	Generate offer letters, track acceptance and manage negotiations.
Talent pool	Retain eligible candidates for future vacancies under an approved retention policy.
Recruitment analytics	Report time to hire, recruitment cost, vacancy ageing and candidate conversion rates.
Internal recruitment	Allow existing employees to apply for internal vacancies.
Advanced capabilities can include career pages, integration with job boards, CV parsing and AI-assisted candidate matching. AI should support—not independently determine—hiring decisions.
4. Employee onboarding, induction and probation
The system should ensure that every new employee is ready to work on time and receives a consistent introduction to the organization.
- Digital offer acceptance and appointment documentation.
- New-hire information and document collection.
- Pre-employment checklist and verification tracking.
- Automated onboarding tasks for HR, the hiring manager, IT, finance and administration.
- Departmental induction and organization policy acknowledgment.
- Role-specific orientation and mandatory training assignments.
- Assignment of workstations, uniforms, equipment, access cards and other resources.
- User account and application access requests, with authorized IT provisioning.
- Probation start dates, review milestones, reminders and evaluation forms.
- Probation confirmation, extension or termination workflows.
- New-hire feedback surveys.
- Onboarding completion dashboard and overdue-task escalation.
Example: When HR registers a new employee, the system automatically creates the onboarding checklist. IT receives an account and equipment request, the department receives an induction task, and HR receives a probation review reminder at the appropriate time.
5. Time, attendance and absence management
This module should help the organization establish accurate attendance records, ensure fair application of policies and provide reliable inputs to payroll.
- Clock-in and clock-out recording.
- Biometric device integration where appropriate.
- Web and mobile attendance, with location controls where justified.
- Shift scheduling, rosters and rotating shift patterns.
- Late arrival, early departure and absence tracking.
- Overtime, night duty, weekend and public holiday calculations.
- Attendance correction requests with approval workflows.
- Timesheets and work-hour summaries.
- Missing clock-in/out detection.
- Public holiday and organization-specific calendar management.
- Attendance exceptions and supervisor approvals.
- Automated attendance summaries for payroll.
- Absence pattern analysis and attendance compliance reports.
- Rules for grace periods, breaks and shift handovers.
The system should support multiple attendance policies by department, employee category, shift and location.
6. Leave and holiday management
Leave management should replace paper applications and manual leave balance calculations with a transparent, auditable process.
Feature	Expected capability
Leave types	Annual, sick, maternity, paternity, compassionate, study, unpaid and other policy-defined leave.
Leave entitlement	Calculate entitlements according to applicable law, employment terms and organizational policy.
Accrual and carryover	Manage monthly or annual accrual, carryover limits and expiry rules.
Leave application	Allow employees to apply online and provide required supporting documentation securely.
Approval workflow	Route requests to the appropriate supervisor and HR according to policy.
Leave balance	Display opening balance, accrued days, approved leave, pending requests and remaining entitlement.
Team leave calendar	Show approved absences to authorized colleagues and managers.
Leave conflicts	Flag overlapping absences or insufficient departmental staffing.
Recall and cancellation	Manage leave cancellation, amendments or recall according to policy.
Return-to-work tracking	Record return dates and follow-up tasks where required.
Leave analytics	Report leave utilization, outstanding balances and absence trends.
A strong implementation should distinguish approved leave from pending requests so that payroll and staffing reports do not use unapproved absence data.
7. Payroll and salary administration
Essential
Payroll is one of the most sensitive and financially important HRMS modules. It should produce accurate, reproducible calculations and provide strong controls over who can change, approve and release payroll.
Payroll processing
- Monthly, weekly or other approved pay cycles.
- Basic salary, grade-based salary scales and step increments.
- Allowances, bonuses, commissions, overtime and other earnings.
- Taxable and non-taxable earning classifications where legally applicable.
- Statutory and voluntary deductions.
- Employee loans, advances and scheduled repayments.
- Pension and retirement contribution calculations.
- Unpaid leave and absence-related adjustments.
- Salary arrears, retroactive adjustments and back pay.
- New-hire, transfer, promotion and termination payroll changes.
- Final salary and termination settlement.
- Gross pay, deductions and net pay calculations.
- Payslip generation and secure distribution.
- Bank payment files and payment reconciliation.
- Payroll journal and finance-system integration.
- Payroll costing by department, unit, project or cost centre.
- Payroll reversal, correction and rerun procedures.
- Payroll locking, approval and period closure.
- Payroll audit trails and variance reports.
Ghana-specific payroll capabilities
For an organization operating in Ghana, assess whether the payroll module supports:
- Current Ghana Revenue Authority PAYE requirements.
- Applicable employee and employer pension contributions, including SSNIT and relevant second-tier arrangements.
- Approved tax reliefs and statutory deductions.
- Applicable labor legislation and current minimum employment requirements.
- Annual tax and payroll reporting.
- Ghana cedi payments, with foreign-currency support if needed.
- Integration with the organization's bank and finance processes.
The software vendor should demonstrate that statutory rules are configurable and updated when regulations change. Confirm the precise requirements with the relevant Ghanaian authorities and payroll professionals before production use.

9. Employee self-service portal (ESS)
Essential
A good HRMS reduces unnecessary visits, phone calls and emails to HR by allowing employees to complete routine tasks themselves.

Employees should be able to:
- View their profile and employment information.
- Submit personal information updates for approval.
- View leave balances, apply for leave and track approvals.
- Check attendance and submit corrections.
- Access payslips and authorized payroll documents.
- Download employment letters and other permitted documents.
- View work schedules, assigned shifts and holiday calendars.
- Submit training requests and review training history.
- View performance objectives and appraisal feedback.
- Update emergency contacts and beneficiary information where permitted.
- Submit HR service requests, questions, complaints and grievances.
- Track the progress of requests and see who must act next.
- Receive HR announcements, reminders and policy updates.
- Access policies and acknowledge required documents.
- Use the portal from a mobile device or desktop.
A manager self-service portal should separately support team attendance, leave approvals, appraisal reviews, onboarding tasks, staffing requests and team-level reports.
10. HR service desk and case management
This is one of the most important modules if the goal is to make HR effective and efficient in service delivery, rather than merely digitizing records.

The HR service desk should operate like a professional support desk, with requests tracked from submission to resolution.
Feature	How it improves service delivery
Service catalogue	Employees choose a defined service such as an employment letter, payroll query or leave correction.
Ticket creation	Assign a unique reference number to each request.
Request categorization	Classify requests by payroll, leave, benefits, recruitment, records or employee relations.
Automatic routing	Assign cases to the appropriate HR officer or specialist team.
Priority and SLA	Define response and resolution targets based on request type and urgency.
Case ownership	Show the responsible officer and the current status.
Escalation rules	Notify supervisors when cases approach or exceed service deadlines.
Internal notes	Allow authorized HR personnel to collaborate privately on a case.
Employee communication	Notify the employee when a request is received, updated or resolved.
Knowledge base	Provide answers to frequently asked HR questions, policies and procedures.
Reopening and feedback	Allow eligible cases to be reopened and employees to rate the service received.
Service analytics	Measure response times, resolution times, backlog, SLA compliance and satisfaction.
Example: An employee requests an employment confirmation letter. The system registers the request, checks the required information, routes it to the responsible HR officer, records approval, generates the letter and notifies the employee when it is ready.

A professional system should support different workflows and deadlines for different service types, rather than treating every request as an identical ticket.
11. Performance management and employee appraisal
Performance management should be a continuous process that connects individual work to organizational objectives.
- Annual, semiannual, quarterly and probationary appraisals.
- Configurable appraisal templates for different job categories.
- Individual objectives and key performance indicators (KPIs).
- Cascading objectives from organizational goals to departments and employees.
- Competency-based evaluations.
- Self-assessment and supervisor assessment.

- Links between performance, promotion, training and compensation decisions, subject to policy.
- Reminders for overdue reviews and incomplete appraisals.
The system should support role-specific assessment criteria. For example, an employee in finance should not be evaluated against the same operational indicators as an IT officer, nurse or procurement officer.

23. Workflow automation and business process management
This is the engine that makes all the other modules work together. Instead of employees carrying forms from office to office, the HRMS should route tasks to the correct people automatically.

Essential features include:
- Configurable multi-level approval workflows.
- Conditional approvals based on department, grade, request type or amount.
- Parallel and sequential approvals.
- Delegation of authority during a manager's absence.
- Automatic task assignment and routing.
- Due dates, SLA timers and escalation rules.
- Email, in-app and optional SMS notifications.
- Reminders for pending tasks.
- Approval, rejection and return-for-correction actions.
- Mandatory comments for defined decisions.
- Separation of duties for sensitive transactions.
- Workflow templates that HR administrators can configure without rewriting software.
- Workflow history showing who acted, when and what decision was made.
- Automated actions triggered by employment events.
- Exception handling when an approver is unavailable or a workflow fails.
Example: Automated leave approval
1. Employee submits leave request

Dates, leave type and available balance are checked.

2. Supervisor reviews

Checks staffing needs and applicable policy.

3. HR validates where required

Additional review follows the organization's approval rules.

4. System updates records

Employee is notified, leave balance is updated and authorized scheduling and payroll processes receive the relevant information.


The same workflow engine should power recruitment, salary changes, staff transfers, employment letters, training requests, grievances and exit clearance.
24. Security, access control and audit management
An HRMS stores highly confidential information. Security must be part of its architecture, not an optional feature.
Security capability	Requirement
Role-based access control (RBAC)	Give each role only the permissions it needs.
Fine-grained permissions	Restrict access by employee, department, field, record or case where needed.
Multi-factor authentication (MFA)	Require additional authentication for sensitive accounts and actions.
Single sign-on (SSO)	Integrate with the organization's identity provider where available.
Segregation of duties	Prevent one person from initiating, approving and completing sensitive transactions without appropriate independent controls.
Audit trails	Record important changes, approvals, exports and administrative actions.
Encryption	Protect data in transit and at rest.
Session controls	Support timeouts, secure sessions and revocation of access.
Data masking	Hide sensitive fields from users without authorization.
Login monitoring	Record failed logins and suspicious access patterns.
Backup and recovery	Support encrypted backups, restoration tests and documented recovery procedures.
Retention and deletion	Enforce approved data retention and lawful disposal policies.
Additional controls should include:
- No shared user accounts for HR officers.
- Restricted access to salary records, bank details, disciplinary cases and health-related information.
- Approval for bulk data exports.
- Monitoring of privileged administrators.
- Secure development, dependency patching and vulnerability management.
- Disaster recovery planning and recovery testing.
- A documented incident response process.
- Data access reviews when staff change roles or leave.
For a professional implementation, audit records should be protected against unauthorized alteration, and sensitive administrative changes should be independently reviewable.

- Single sign-on and identity provider integration.

- Biometric attendance device integration.

29. Artificial intelligence and intelligent automation
AI is an optional advanced capability, not a substitute for sound HR processes or accurate records.

Potential features include:
- HR policy chatbot that answers questions from approved internal documents.
- Natural-language search across authorized HR records.
- Automated classification and routing of HR requests.
- Drafting of standard HR correspondence.
- Summarization of non-sensitive case notes for authorized reviewers.
- Recruitment assistance and job description drafting.
- Skills gap identification and training recommendations.
- Workforce forecasting and anomaly detection.
- Natural-language reporting, such as asking for headcount by department.
- Alerts for unusual payroll variations or data inconsistencies.
AI governance requirements: Use permissions-aware retrieval, protect employee information from unauthorized disclosure, validate generated outputs, retain human approval for consequential decisions, and document how AI is used. Do not let an unreviewed model independently decide hiring, dismissal, promotion, disciplinary outcomes or employee compensation.

- Handover and clearance: Coordinate departmental handover, asset return and exit clearance.
Hospital integrations
A hospital HRMS may also integrate with:
- Payroll and accounting systems.
- Biometric attendance terminals.
- Active Directory or another identity management platform.
- Hospital information systems, such as LHIMS, where an appropriate integration exists.
- Electronic rostering or workforce scheduling tools.
- Learning management systems.
- IT service management and asset inventory systems.
Patient records should not be copied into the HRMS unnecessarily. Integrations should use the minimum employee or workforce data needed, with strict access boundaries.
32. Non-functional requirements: What makes the software enterprise-grade?
A system can have dozens of modules and still be unsuitable for professional use if it is unreliable, insecure or difficult to maintain.
Requirement	What to specify
Reliability	Defined uptime target, monitoring and incident procedures.
Performance	Responsive searches, dashboards, forms and payroll processing under expected load.
Scalability	Ability to add employees, departments, locations and users without major redesign.
Availability	Documented maintenance windows and recovery procedures.
Backup and recovery	Automated backups, protected copies, tested restores, and defined recovery objectives.
Security	MFA, least privilege, encryption, auditing and vulnerability management.
Data integrity	Database constraints, transaction consistency and duplicate prevention.
Maintainability	Documented architecture, versioned configurations, testing and upgrade procedures.
Portability	Data export in usable formats and a documented migration process.
Accessibility	Usable interface for employees with different abilities.
Localization	Ghana cedi, relevant date formats, public holidays and configurable local policies.
Documentation	User guides, administrator manuals, API documentation and operational runbooks.
Supportability	Defined support channels, incident priorities and vendor escalation procedures.
Environment separation	Separate development, testing/staging and production environments.
For a hospital, I would also require a documented disaster recovery plan and tested recovery procedures. Define recovery time objective (RTO) and recovery point objective (RPO) based on the impact of losing access to HR and payroll services.
33. User roles and permissions
The HRMS should have separate, configurable permissions for different responsibilities.
Role	Typical access
System administrator	Technical configuration, user management and system health; sensitive HR data access should be separately controlled.
HR director / HR manager	Broad authorized oversight, HR approvals and management reporting.
HR officer	Employee records, personnel transactions and assigned HR workflows.
Recruitment officer	Vacancies, applications, interviews and recruitment reports.
Payroll officer	Salary calculations, deductions and payroll preparation.
Payroll approver	Independent payroll review, approval and authorized release.
Department head	Team records, attendance, leave, appraisals and staffing information within their scope.
Line manager	Assigned employee requests, objectives, appraisals and approvals.
Finance officer	Authorized payroll costing, accounting outputs and financial reconciliation.
Training officer	Training schedules, attendance, qualifications and development records.
Employee	Own profile, leave, payslips, service requests and other permitted self-service features.
Internal auditor	Read-only access to assigned audit records, transactions and reports.
Occupational health professional	Specifically authorized occupational health records, separated from general HR access.
The exact roles should reflect the organization's governance structure. A user should not receive access to confidential information merely because they hold a senior title.
34. HR service delivery KPIs
To make HR genuinely effective, the HRMS must measure the quality and speed of its services—not simply count how many records it stores.

Here are the indicators I would put on the HR director's dashboard.

Service efficiency
- Average time to resolve an HR request.
- Percentage of requests completed within SLA.
- Number of overdue requests.
- Average approval turnaround time.
- Requests resolved per HR officer.




Accuracy and compliance
- Payroll error rate.
- Percentage of employee records complete and verified.
- Percentage of required licenses and training current.
- Percentage of appraisals completed on time.
- Number of unresolved audit findings.




Workforce effectiveness
- Staff turnover and retention.
- Vacancy rate and time to hire.
- Absence rate and overtime trends.
- Training completion and effectiveness.
- Employee satisfaction with HR services.




Digital service adoption
- Percentage of requests submitted through self-service.
- Percentage of HR processes automated.
- Reduction in paper-based transactions.
- Percentage of workflows completed without manual intervention.
- Employee self-service usage rate.



Every KPI should have a defined formula, data source, reporting frequency, accountable owner and target. Targets should be established from a baseline rather than chosen arbitrarily.
35. How I would prioritize development or implementation
A professional HRMS does not need to launch every module on day one. It should establish a reliable foundation first, then expand according to business value and operational readiness.
1

Phase 1 — Core operations
Highest priority
Employee records, organization structure, document management, leave, attendance, role-based access, approval workflows, employee self-service and essential reporting.



2

Phase 2 — Financial and service automation

Payroll integration or processing, benefits, HR service desk, automated letters, notifications, audit reports and finance integration.



3

Phase 3 — Talent and workforce management

Recruitment, onboarding, probation, performance appraisal, training, staff scheduling, employee relations and offboarding.



4

Phase 4 — Enterprise intelligence

Advanced workforce analytics, establishment planning, succession planning, predictive reporting, integrations, mobile enhancements and governed AI capabilities.



Payroll can move into Phase 1 if it is the organization's biggest operational problem. However, if payroll is already handled reliably by another system, integrating it may be safer and more cost-effective than replacing it immediately.
